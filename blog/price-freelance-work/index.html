How to Price Your Freelance Work Without Undervaluing Yourself

Most freelancers undercharge because they price from fear — fear of losing the client, fear of sounding expensive, fear of comparing themselves to cheaper options. The fix is not “charge more.” The fix is a clear pricing system.

Start with your floor rate

Calculate what you need to earn monthly (bills, tools, taxes, savings), then divide by realistic billable hours — usually 60–70% of your working time. That number is your floor. Never go below it.

If a project pays less than your floor after revisions and meetings, it is not a client — it is a donation.

Price the outcome, not just the hours

Hourly rates are easy to compare and easy to race to the bottom. Wherever possible, package work by outcome: a landing page that converts, a brand kit ready for launch, a WordPress site ready for content.

  • Define deliverables clearly
  • Include a revision limit
  • Add rush fees for tight timelines
  • Separate maintenance from build work

Use three tiers

Offer Essential, Standard, and Premium packages. Most clients choose the middle option — and you stop negotiating against a single number.

Raise rates with proof

After every strong project, review your portfolio, testimonials, and process. Increase rates when demand is steady, not when you feel desperate. Confidence follows systems.
